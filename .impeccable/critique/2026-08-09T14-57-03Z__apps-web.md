## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment**: Genuinely authored for this product, not a reskinned CRUD generator. The facilitador grace-period pattern, the entity-name-resolution rule, the diagnostic scoring domain logic, and the 84/84 specific error messages are real domain modeling, not boilerplate. But brand discipline is only consistently enforced in admin CRUD screens — the two most consequential end-user surfaces (the student-facing diagnostic form and the workshop canvas) break the navy/flat/no-shadow system wholesale, introducing a second "Google Forms" teal aesthetic and a rainbow sticky-note board.

**Deterministic scan**: 17 findings across 3 rules — `side-tab` (13), `border-accent-on-rounded` (2), `layout-transition` (2). The two `layout-transition` hits (progress-bar fill animations) are likely low-severity false positives — narrow decorative elements, not page-level reflow risk.

## Overall Impression

This is a well-engineered, domain-specific application with genuinely strong accessible primitives (`Field`, `Modal`) and best-in-class error copy (84/84 codes, perfect es/pt parity) — but its own design discipline is unevenly enforced, and the two screens a paying client's employees actually touch (the AI-diagnostic form and the client results portal) are the weakest, most inconsistent, and highest-risk parts of the whole product. The single biggest opportunity: bring the student-facing diagnostic surface back into the navy/flat system and close the silent-failure gap in the client portal — both are P0 because they sit directly in front of the people DAnalytics is delivering the service to.

## What's Working

- **`Field.tsx` and `Modal.tsx`** are genuinely well-engineered accessible primitives (auto-wired `aria-describedby`/`aria-invalid`, real focus trap + restoration + Escape-to-close), not boilerplate — they lift the accessibility floor of the entire app.
- **The facilitador grace-period + entity-name-resolution pattern** is the project's own documented reference implementation and is followed with real discipline: no raw UUIDs ever surface, and read-only degradation after grace expiry is visible and explained, never a silent block.
- **`errors.json`'s completeness**: 84/84 `AppErrorCode` values covered with specific, actionable copy, zero key drift across es/pt — unusually mature for MVP stage, confirmed independently by both assessments (design review + detector's rule-count corroboration of the systemic `.card-accent` pattern shows the same care extends to CSS tokens, just inconsistently applied at the edges).

## Priority Issues

**[P0] `RunnerPage.tsx` bypasses the app's entire error-handling contract on the highest-stakes screen.**
Why it matters: This is where the client's employees actually do the paid diagnostic/workshop. The file imports `fetchWithErrorMapping` but never calls it — all 16 network calls use raw `fetch`, and one path shows a raw, unmapped backend message directly to the user.
Fix: Route every call through `fetchWithErrorMapping`/`translateError`; remove the raw-message fallback.
Suggested command: `/impeccable harden`

**[P0] `PortalProgramaDetallePage.tsx` (the client's view into the service they paid for) fails silently with no wayfinding.**
Why it matters: Breadcrumb/PageHeader are nested inside the data-present conditional, so there's zero chrome during loading, and no error/retry path if the fetch fails — just a permanently blank page. This is a paying client contact's primary visibility into their investment.
Fix: Hoist Breadcrumb/PageHeader outside the conditional (matching every other page); add an `<Alert variant="danger">` + retry on fetch failure.
Suggested command: `/impeccable harden`

**[P1] The student-facing diagnostic form and workshop canvas break the design system wholesale.**
Why it matters: `CanvasGrid.tsx` and the `.gform-*` subsystem (confirmed independently by the detector: both `border-accent-on-rounded` hits and several `side-tab` hits land here) introduce hardcoded rainbow palettes, shadows, and non-zero radius — three of the system's hardest rules broken at once, in the two screens actually seen by a paying client's employees.
Fix: Restyle using existing status/accent tokens and flat/radius-0 conventions; color can still differentiate without shadows or rounding.
Suggested command: `/impeccable adapt`

**[P1] `ProgramasPage.tsx` is a 2156-line monolith: drawer-over-modals-over-page for the single most-used admin workflow.**
Why it matters: Nested overlay stacking (drawer + tabs + further modals) is a real cognitive-load and focus-management risk for the admin running the whole operation, not just a code-hygiene issue.
Fix: Split into separate files per sub-component; consider promoting the detail drawer's tabs to full sub-routes instead of stacking modals on top.
Suggested command: `/impeccable distill`

**[P2] No confirmation before irreversible diagnostic submission; icon-only control with no accessible name in `FormularioResponderPage.tsx`.**
Why it matters: The product's own rules explicitly design for a "slow, doesn't reread the screen" student persona, and confirms far lower-stakes actions elsewhere — a one-way submit with zero warning contradicts that philosophy.
Fix: Add `ConfirmModal` before final submit; add `aria-label` to the icon-only remove-row button.
Suggested command: `/impeccable clarify`

## Persona Red Flags

**Alex (power-user admin running the whole operation)**: Loses page-level context after 2-3 nested overlays in `ProgramasPage.tsx` with no breadcrumb trail inside the drawer. `RegistroAccesoPage.tsx` gives no way to audit "what did user X do" without already knowing their UUID — a dead end for exactly the investigative task an audit log exists for. `Pagination.tsx` renders one button per page with no truncation, so any list with more than a couple dozen pages becomes a wall of buttons.

**Jordan (first-time estudiante — the persona this product's own rules explicitly target as "slow, doesn't reread")**: Submits the irreversible AI-diagnostic form with no warning it can't be edited after. Uploads a final deliverable with a static "subiendo…" label and no progress bar near a deadline, with a generic, non-specific error on failure.

**Sam (accessibility-dependent user)**: `toast-store.tsx` announces both success and error toasts with `role="status"` — an error toast gets the same passive announcement priority as success, so it can be missed entirely (the correct pattern, `role="alert"` for danger, already exists two files away in `Alert.tsx`, just not applied here). `DataTable.tsx` has no `scope="col"` or table caption/aria-label on any admin data table.
